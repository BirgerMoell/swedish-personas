Swedish Personas v2: how the data was made

Renaissance Labs · 100,000 synthetic Swedish residents in the Nemotron-Personas schema · open under CC BY 4.0 (code MIT) · huggingface.co/RenaissanceLabsEU/swedish-personas-v2

What it is and why

A synthetic population that mirrors Sweden's statistics: every person is a full record of demographics, work, personality, health and written life stories. It is meant for training and evaluating Swedish language models, and each person has a work profile linked to a catalogue of real work tasks, so reinforcement-learning environments can later be built on the tasks that dominate each occupation. Version 1 was criticised by an outside reviewer for templated text, small name pools, occupation unrelated to education, and no regional or health structure. Version 2 answers each point.

Pipeline: numbers come from statistics, words come from a model

1. Statistics, not a modelAge, sex, county, municipality, country of birth, household, education, occupation, industry and employment are drawn from Statistics Sweden (SCB) tables. Several conditional tables are combined so that, for example, occupation depends on education and age.
2. Names, personality, healthNames come from large name pools matched to background. Big Five scores are drawn per person. Health conditions are drawn from prevalence by age and sex, with a shared latent factor so that conditions co-occur realistically.
3. Work layerThe official occupation description (JobTech Taxonomy) is turned by the model into 8–12 tasks per occupation. Each worker gets a job title, workplace and 4–7 of those tasks with a share of the week.
4. Life storiesGemma 4 31B writes 11 text fields per person from the facts above. Rules forbid changing any fact, ban clichés, and give each field a randomly drawn opening style and leisure ideas to prevent sameness.

Results so far and what is open

Variety: repeated five-word phrases in the persona field fell from 14.4% in v1 to 0.04% in a 987-person model-written pilot. Work layer: 2,004 tasks for 197 occupation groups. Status: structured data for all 100,000 people is released; the model-written text for all 100,000 started on Berzelius on 24 November and the catalogue is being extended to all 428 occupation groups.

Known limits. Text, tasks and tools are model-written and have been reviewed only by sampling; name-heritage labels are partly wrong; the data holds no year of arrival, so immigration stories are the model's choice; the people are synthetic and fit no real individual.
